S.I.E.R.R.A has applied for a competitive grant from the Calaveras Community Foundation. If the application is funded, the money would pay for ten solar-powered mesh repeaters placed along the Highway 4 corridor and a series of free public workshops on off-grid communication. An application is not an award: the foundation's decision is expected later this year.

## Who the foundation is

The [Calaveras Community Foundation](https://calaverascommunityfoundation.org/about-ccf/) is a 501(c)(3) public charity that has distributed more than $3.7 million in competitive grants, scholarships, and assistance across the county since it was formed in 2000. It runs a competitive grant cycle each year for local nonprofits, schools, and government units; in 2024 it [awarded grants to 29 organizations](https://www.uniondemocrat.com/news/article_492c9c68-873a-11ef-abdb-375475623de0.html). This application is one entry in that cycle, not a commitment from the foundation.

## What the grant would fund

The proposal is for ten solar-powered repeaters, sited at points along the corridor from the Angels Camp foothills up through Murphys and Arnold toward Bear Valley. The reason to build there is the terrain: the corridor climbs from oak foothills into timbered high country, ground where cell coverage thins by nature and where winter storms and Public Safety Power Shutoffs regularly pull the grid down.

The repeaters would run on [MeshCore](https://github.com/meshcore-dev/MeshCore), the platform this network is built on. MeshCore is a LoRa mesh — LoRa being the long-range, low-power radio the relays use to pass short text messages hop by hop when there is no cell tower in the path. Its design leans on a small number of deliberately placed, maintained repeaters holding known routes between them, rather than a dense swarm of cheap nodes. That architecture is chosen for ridgeline country, where a few well-sited relays with line of sight down a canyon do more than many low ones scattered without it. Ten repeaters is a count of the hardware a grant would buy. Where a signal actually reaches down a given canyon is something no application can settle in advance.

## The workshops

The other half of the proposal is education. S.I.E.R.R.A plans a run of free public workshops introducing residents to off-grid messaging: what a mesh network is, how it differs from Wi-Fi and cellular, and how an inexpensive LoRa handheld pairs with a phone to send text when the usual network is down. The sessions are aimed at families, neighborhood groups, and anyone who wants a communications option that does not route through a carrier.

That order — teach first, then build — is the alliance's stated doctrine: Educate, Build, Operate, with the resilience meant to be in place before the season that needs it, not improvised during one. It is the same logic behind looking up an evacuation zone or clearing defensible space in an ordinary month rather than a dangerous one.

The workshop dates and any node plans depend on the grant, and follow only if it lands. Residents who want to hear about them first — or to help build toward them either way — can reach the alliance through the [contact page](/contact).
