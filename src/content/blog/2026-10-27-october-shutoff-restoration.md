Households along the Highway 4 and Highway 108 corridors lost power on the evening of October 14, and some stayed without it until October 17. Power does not return when the wind stops. It returns after every de-energized line has been inspected.

## What happened

A Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) is PG&E turning off power lines ahead of strong wind, so a damaged line cannot start a fire. On October 14, PG&E [put about 88,000 customers in 29 counties in scope](https://investor.pgecorp.com/news-events/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/PGE-Urges-Customers-to-Prepare-as-Planned-Public-Safety-Power-Shutoff-Approaches/default.aspx), including an estimated 10,793 in Calaveras County and 7,248 in Tuolumne County.

In the Mother Lode, [outages began in the 9 and 10 o'clock hours Wednesday evening](https://mymotherlode.com/news/local/11223694/planned-power-outages-underway-across-mother-lode.html) in places including Arnold, West Point, Railroad Flat, Twain Harte, Mi Wuk Village, Long Barn, and Pinecrest. At the peak, [10,700 customers in Calaveras and 7,200 in Tuolumne](https://mymotherlode.com/news/local/11229434/pge-updates-remain-power-shutoffs-in-mother-lode.html) were without power. PG&E's meteorologists [recorded gusts up to 67 mph](https://www.pge.com/en/newsroom/currents/safety/pge-may-need-to-turn-off-power-for-safety.html) during the event.

## Why the power stayed off

Before power goes back on, the lines are inspected. PG&E's crews [waited until the winds passed, then began patrols at 12:30 p.m. on October 16](https://www.pge.com/en/newsroom/currents/safety/pge-may-need-to-turn-off-power-for-safety.html) in portions of 19 counties, inspecting every de-energized line and piece of equipment before restoring it. Those inspections found broken equipment and branches and trees in contact with lines, and each had to be repaired before that section came back.

Tuolumne County customers were all restored on October 16. In Calaveras County, crews [found a damaged section of line in the Wilseyville area](https://mymotherlode.com/news/local/11229434/pge-updates-remain-power-shutoffs-in-mother-lode.html), and the last 185 customers there stayed dark overnight until the repair was finished, around 10 a.m. on Saturday, October 17. For them, the outage ran from Wednesday night to Saturday morning. PG&E's own guidance says outages [may last "several hours or several days"](https://www.pge.com/en/outages-and-safety/safety/community-wildfire-safety-program/public-safety-power-shutoffs.html) depending on the weather and the time needed to restore power safely.

## Three things to set up before the next one

**Plan for the tail.** Budget phone, radio, and battery-bank charge for the day after the wind as well as the windy night. In this event the first patrols began on Friday afternoon, after two nights, and one damaged section of line kept a neighborhood without power until Saturday morning.

**Add the addresses you check on.** PG&E account holders get PSPS notices automatically. [Address Alerts](https://www.pge.com/en/outages-and-safety/safety/community-wildfire-safety-program/public-safety-power-shutoffs.html) send phone and text notices for any other address, such as a parent's house in Arnold or a cabin above Dorrington. Notice can be short: PG&E says that in some cases the first alert may not go out until the same day the power is shut off.

**Know where the charging is.** PG&E opens [Community Resource Centers](https://pgealerts.alerts.pge.com/ways-we-can-help/) during a shutoff, and PG&E says every center offers device charging, medical equipment charging, and Wi-Fi. In October, the local centers [included Chapel in the Pines on Highway 4 in Arnold, West Point Community Hall, Eproson Park in Twain Harte, and Word of Life Fellowship on Highway 108](https://mymotherlode.com/news/local/11223694/planned-power-outages-underway-across-mother-lode.html), open 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. on Thursday and Friday. Locations change from event to event, and PG&E says openings and updates appear on its [Outage Center map](https://pgealerts.alerts.pge.com/ways-we-can-help/) once a shutoff begins.
