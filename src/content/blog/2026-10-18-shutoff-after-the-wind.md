PG&E's shutoff this month left about 18,000 customers in Calaveras and Tuolumne without power for up to three days. Power stays off until crews have inspected the lines, so a shutoff runs well past the weather that prompted it. A household comms plan has to cover the whole outage.

## What happened

PG&E began cutting power on the evening of Wednesday, October 14, ahead of a dry, windy system. By Friday morning, when the last circuits were switched off, [about 85,900 customers in 29 counties](https://www.pge.com/en/newsroom/currents/safety/pge-may-need-to-turn-off-power-for-safety.html) had lost power. That included [about 10,790 customers in Calaveras and 7,248 in Tuolumne](https://kioncentralcoast.com/news/2026/10/13/pge-eyes-another-northern-california-power-shutoff-after-weekend-psps/). PG&E opened more than 40 Community Resource Centers across the affected counties.

Calaveras and Tuolumne customers had been restored by Saturday afternoon. [PG&E's final update](https://www.pge.com/en/newsroom/currents/safety/pge-may-need-to-turn-off-power-for-safety.html), at 7:20 p.m. on Saturday, October 17, reported virtually all customers restored statewide, about 74 hours after the first circuits went off.

## Why restoration takes days

PG&E does not switch the lines back on when the wind drops. After an area is declared clear, [crews patrol nearly every de-energized line by air, by vehicle, and on foot](https://www.turnto23.com/news/state/pg-e-to-begin-patrolling-power-lines-amid-psps-events), looking for fallen branches and damaged equipment. A line comes back only after it has been inspected and found free of hazards. Where a crew finds damage, that section stays off until it is repaired.

## The 72-hour mark

A [2020 California Public Utilities Commission decision](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/internet-and-phone/network-performance-and-public-safety/communications-network-resiliency/communications-resiliency-plans) requires wireless carriers to keep cell sites in the state's Tier 2 and Tier 3 high fire-threat districts running on backup power for at least 72 hours after the grid goes down. Both counties sit in [those districts](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/wildfires/fire-threat-maps-and-fire-safety-rulemaking). This month's outage ran close to that limit. A longer event, or one where repairs hold a circuit off, would run past it, and from then on a cell site with no grid power has no required backup. ([An earlier post](/blog/2026-07-17-psps-backup-power-deadline/) covers the rule in more detail.)

## What to have ready

Each of these has to be in place before the power goes off:

- **Power for three days.** Battery banks and a vehicle charger, sized to keep a phone and a handheld radio running through the whole outage.
- **A radio that needs no grid.** A battery or hand-crank NOAA Weather Radio receives National Weather Service broadcasts directly, with no cell site or account involved.
- **Restoration times treated as estimates.** PG&E's posted restoration times are projections. Plan around the longer end.
