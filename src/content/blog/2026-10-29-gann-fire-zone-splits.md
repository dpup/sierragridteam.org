Calaveras County asks residents to know their evacuation zone and to write it down somewhere easy to find. During the Gann Fire in August, several zones were divided into lettered parts while the fire was still burning, and each part carried its own status.

## The fire

The Gann Fire started on August 3 at 5:27 p.m. near Hogan Dam Road and Gann Road in Valley Springs, according to [CAL FIRE's incident page](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2026/8/3/gann-fire). It burned 10,339 acres, destroyed 48 structures, and damaged 3 more, with one confirmed civilian fatality. CAL FIRE's Tuolumne-Calaveras Unit ran the fire in unified command with the Calaveras County Sheriff's Office, and CAL FIRE declared it 100% contained on August 12. The figures here are CAL FIRE's final ones.

## How the zones changed

Calaveras County [divides the county into pre-established evacuation zones](https://www.calaverasgov.us/Quick-Links/Know-Your-Zone) through Genasys Protect, and alerts name those zones. Evacuation orders went out on the first evening. On August 5 at 3:09 p.m., CAL FIRE posted: "Zone CCU-110 has been split. CCU-110-A remains in Evacuation Order status." The other part, CCU-110-B, dropped to an evacuation warning. One zone number now covered two different instructions.

That pattern repeated as the fire settled. CAL FIRE's [August 9 morning update](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2026/8/3/gann-fire/updates/d3d81661-1823-4ec9-8eb7-dd6306b3ce5e) listed CCU-109-B under an order and CCU-109-A under a warning, and zones 130 and 147 were divided the same way. By the [morning of August 10](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2026/8/3/gann-fire/updates/233def7e-eb75-4cf4-bdf6-e3830b0c6e22), the zones still under orders were CCU-109-C, CCU-130-D, CCU-139-D, and CCU-147-D. By then, some zone numbers carried letters as far as D.

The orders were lifted in steps. Residents were allowed back to properties inside the fire zones at 8:00 a.m. on August 8. On August 9, all warnings were lifted and orders were kept "in the interior of the fire only." At 5:00 p.m. on August 10, CAL FIRE reported that "All Evacuation Orders and Traffic Control Points were lifted."

## Going home without power

Power did not return everywhere when residents did. CAL FIRE's August 6 update said "utility crews are still in the area working to restore service as quickly and safely as possible," and that some properties might stay without power after residents returned. On August 10, the same day the last orders came off, it reported that PG&E "continues working to repair infrastructure and restore power to affected areas."

## For the next evacuation

- **Keep the zone number, and know how to look it up.** A lettered zone such as CCU-110-A is part of zone CCU-110, and it can carry a different status from the rest of that zone. When an update names lettered zones, look up your address on the county's zone map through [Genasys Protect](https://protect.genasys.com/) and check it against the current update, instead of matching the number from memory.
- **Go to the source CAL FIRE named.** CAL FIRE's Gann Fire updates sent readers to the [Calaveras County Sheriff's Office](https://sheriff.calaverasgov.us/) for current evacuation information and alert sign-up. The county asks residents to register for [Calaveras Alert](https://www.calaverasgov.us/Quick-Links/Know-Your-Zone).
- **Plan the return as well as the departure.** A household that returns when an order lifts may still be without power. Food, water, light, and a way to charge a phone belong in the go-bag for the trip home as well.
