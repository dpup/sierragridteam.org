Through the back half of a foothill summer, two phrases show up in the forecast often enough to blur together: Fire Weather Watch and Red Flag Warning. They are not the same alert, and the difference is the useful part. This week is a live example — the National Weather Service has the Sierra foothills under [elevated fire weather conditions](https://www.cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/northern-california-heat-advisory-aug-8-2026/) alongside a heat advisory, with valley and foothill highs of 102 to 108 through Sunday. Elevated is the middle rung. Here is the rest of the ladder.

## Watch versus Warning

A [Fire Weather Watch](https://www.weather.gov/safety/wildfire-ww) is about potential. The weather service issues one when the ingredients for dangerous fire weather — some combination of low humidity, wind, and dried-out fuel — look likely to come together in the next day or two, but are not here yet. It is a heads-up with lead time attached.

A Red Flag Warning is about the present. The weather service issues it, in its own words, ["to alert land managers to an ongoing or imminent critical fire weather pattern"](https://www.weather.gov/safety/wildfire-ww) — the conditions are happening now, or will within hours. A Watch can firm up into a Warning as forecasters gain confidence, or expire without one if the wind never arrives.

Neither is a prediction that a fire will start. Both describe weather that would let a new start spread fast and burn hot: dry air pulling moisture out of the grass and brush, wind to carry embers past a control line. The exact thresholds are set locally. [NWS Sacramento](https://www.weather.gov/sto), the office that forecasts for Calaveras and Tuolumne, keys its warnings to the low humidity and sustained wind its forecasters know move fire in this terrain.

## Where you already see it

The [Live Feed](/live) carries a fire-weather status on the same ladder the weather service uses: Normal, Elevated, Red Flag. When it reads Red Flag, it is mirroring an active NWS Red Flag Warning for the area — not a judgment of our own. The authoritative product, the one that names which zones and until when, lives with [NWS Sacramento](https://www.weather.gov/sto). The Live Feed points to it; the weather service is the source.

Of the two alerts, the Watch is the one with room to act in it. It is the window before the weather arrives — time to top off water, check the generator fuel, and settle how the household will reach each other if the power goes out and the cell bars follow it down. A Red Flag Warning, by the time it posts, is confirmation that the window has closed and the weather is doing what it was forecast to do.
