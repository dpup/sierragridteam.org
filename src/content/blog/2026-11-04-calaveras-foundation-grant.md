The Calaveras Community Foundation has awarded S.I.E.R.R.A a grant. The money goes toward running and expanding the alliance's solar-powered radio network along the Highway 4 corridor, and toward public seminars on how to use it.

The award follows the [application described on this blog in July](/blog/2026-07-29-calaveras-grant-application).

## The foundation

The [Calaveras Community Foundation](https://calaverascommunityfoundation.org/about-ccf/) is a 501(c)(3) public charity. Its stated mission is to improve Calaveras communities by making grants to partner organizations, assisting donors and leading on charitable causes. It was formed in January 2000, and by its own count it has distributed more than $3.77 million since then through competitive grants, scholarships and assistance to local causes.

## What the grant supports

S.I.E.R.R.A is a nonprofit that builds, installs and maintains solar-powered radio equipment in Calaveras County. The grant covers three kinds of work: keeping the existing network running, adding more solar-powered repeaters along the Highway 4 corridor, and teaching residents to use the equipment. S.I.E.R.R.A currently operates 12 solar-powered repeaters along the corridor.

The network runs on [MeshCore](https://github.com/meshcore-dev/MeshCore), open-source firmware for small, low-power LoRa radios that pass short text messages from one radio to the next. A repeater receives a message and transmits it again. A resident's own radio is a "companion" device: it sends and receives its owner's messages but does not relay anyone else's. It can connect to a phone app over Bluetooth, so the phone serves as the screen and keyboard while the radio does the transmitting. The project describes the network as needing no central server or internet connection. Sending or receiving a message on the alliance's network does not use cell service, internet access or a phone line, and the network is free for anyone to use.

## Seminars in Murphys

S.I.E.R.R.A plans to hold hands-on seminars in Murphys on getting, setting up and using MeshCore equipment. Dates have not been announced. Later plans include demonstrating the equipment to local first-responder agencies.

## How to help

Expanding the network also depends on public support and on permission from landowners to install equipment on high ground. Donations through the [donate page](/donate) go toward hardware, battery storage upgrades and high-elevation installations. Landowners who would consider hosting equipment, and anyone who wants to hear when the seminars are scheduled, can reach the alliance through the [contact page](/contact) or at info@sierragridteam.org.

The alliance's [live page](/live) gathers official wildfire, evacuation, weather and road feeds for the Calaveras and Tuolumne foothills in one place.
