Households that keep a pair of two-way radios for days when cell service is thin or the power is out should know that a radio bought online is not always approved for the service it is sold for. A short code on the label, the FCC ID, lets a buyer check that before the radio goes into the kit.

## What changed at eBay this month

An FCC order on equipment authorization took [effect on October 13, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/11/2026-18535/protecting-against-national-security-threats-to-the-communications-supply-chain-through-the). Among other things, it makes clear that the FCC's marketing rules reach online marketplaces that sell unauthorized equipment, and it requires those marketplaces, with limited exceptions, "to display a device's FCC ID at the online point of sale."

eBay has [responded with new listing rules](https://www.arrl.org/news/ebay-restricts-listings-of-radio-equipment-but-exempts-ham-band-only-gear), according to ARRL, the national association for amateur radio. Sellers of transmitting equipment that needs FCC authorization must now submit its FCC ID and show it with the listing. Listings already online, small sellers, used equipment, and gear that works only on amateur frequencies are exempt. An eBay spokesperson told ARRL that two-way radios on the VHF and UHF bands (the ranges handhelds use) that can transmit outside the amateur bands "cannot be imported, advertised, sold, or operated within the United States" without FCC certification.

Because of those exemptions, a radio listing may still show no FCC ID, and a buyer has to find and check it themselves.

## What the FCC ID tells you

Equipment approved through FCC certification must [carry a label with its FCC ID](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-2/subpart-J/section-2.925), legible without magnification and visible at the time of purchase. [The first 3 or 5 characters](https://wiki.radioreference.com/index.php/FCC_ID) identify the company that holds the approval, and the rest identify the product. Entering the full ID in the [FCC's equipment authorization search](https://www.fcc.gov/oet/ea/fccid) brings up the grant, the FCC's approval record, which [defines the frequency ranges and output power](https://ib-lenhardt.com/kb/fcc-requirements/fcc-id) the device was approved for.

For the General Mobile Radio Service (GMRS), the licensed service a household can use for handheld radios, the rules are specific:

- [Each GMRS transmitter must be certified](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-95/subpart-E/section-95.1761) for GMRS.
- The FCC will not certify a radio for GMRS if it can also transmit in services that need no certification, "such as the Amateur Radio Service." A handheld that covers both the ham bands and the GMRS channels is not a certified GMRS radio.
- With narrow exceptions, [operating a transmitter that is not certified](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-95/subpart-A/section-95.335) for a Personal Radio Service "voids the user's authority to operate that station."

## Amateur-only radios

A radio built to transmit only on the amateur bands is a different case. ARRL notes that amateur equipment, apart from commercial high-frequency amplifiers, "continues to not require FCC authorization," which is why eBay exempts it. Such a radio may carry no FCC ID at all. The authority to transmit on it comes from the operator's license, starting with a Technician license that [requires passing a 35-question exam](http://www.arrl.org/getting-your-technician-license).

## Before you buy

1. Ask for the FCC ID if the listing does not show it, and look it up in the FCC search.
2. For a family GMRS radio, confirm the grant approves it for GMRS and that the radio does not also transmit on the ham bands.
3. Check the label on the radio when it arrives against the ID you looked up.
4. Get the license. A GMRS license costs [$35](https://www.fcc.gov/personal-service-and-amateur-application-fees), runs [ten years, and covers immediate family members](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-95/subpart-E/section-95.1705).
